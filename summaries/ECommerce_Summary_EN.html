01 Introduction to Electronic Commerce & Fundamentals (Lecture 1)

Electronic Commerce (EC) represents digitally enabled commercial transactions between and among organizations and individuals. It transforms traditional business through telecommunications and the Internet.

1.1 Diverse Perspectives of E-Commerce

Perspective Core Definition & Scope
Commercial (Trading) Provides the capability of buying, selling, and exchanging products, services, and information over computer networks.
Business Process Doing business electronically by completing workflows over networks, substituting digital information for physical processes.
Service A tool that addresses the desire of firms, consumers, and management to cut service costs while improving the quality of goods and increasing delivery speed.
Collaborative & Learning Enables online collaborative work, inter-organizational learning, information sharing, and global team coordination.

1.2 Major Classifications by Market Relationship

  • B2C (Business-to-Consumer): Online businesses selling merchandise or services directly to individual consumers (e.g. Amazon, Walmart.com). Highly visible to the public.
  • B2B (Business-to-Business): Businesses selling to other businesses (e.g. Grainger, Boeing parts procurement). Accounts for the vast majority of overall e-commerce dollar volume.
  • C2C (Consumer-to-Consumer): Consumers selling directly to other consumers, facilitated by a platform (e.g. eBay auctions, Craigslist, Etsy).
  • C2B (Consumer-to-Business): Consumers setting prices or requirements and businesses competing to satisfy them (e.g. Priceline's "Name Your Own Price", reverse auctions).
  • Intra-business EC (B2E - Business-to-Employee): Internal corporate portal activities, training, benefits selection, and employee self-service on private intranets.
  • Non-business EC: Religious, academic, non-profit institutions, and social agencies using the Internet to reduce costs and accept contributions.
  • B2G & G2C: Business-to-Government (public e-procurement) and Government-to-Citizen (online tax filing, renewing driver's licenses).

1.3 Pure vs. Partial E-Commerce (The 3 Dimensions)

EC is classified along 3 dimensions: (1) The Product/Service, (2) The Business Process, and (3) The Delivery Agent/Intermediary.

  • Traditional Brick-and-Mortar: All 3 dimensions are physical (physical store, cash payment, physical product).
  • Partial E-Commerce: At least one dimension is digital while others are physical. Example: buying a physical paperback book online on Amazon and having FedEx deliver it.
  • Pure E-Commerce: All 3 dimensions are entirely digital. Example: buying and downloading an ebook, software license, or streaming music directly online.

1.4 Benefits and Limitations of E-Commerce

Dimension Key Benefits Limitations & Barriers
For Organizations Global market reach, significant cost reductions, 24/7 continuous operations, rapid time to market, mass customization, and supply chain disintermediation. Technical: Bandwidth constraints, software integration with legacy ERP systems, evolving security standards.
For Consumers Unmatched convenience, massive product selection, instantaneous price comparison, customer reviews, and immediate delivery of digital goods. Non-Technical: Lack of trust, privacy and security fears, inability to physically inspect items before purchase, resistance to change.
For Society Enables telecommuting (reducing traffic congestion and pollution), delivers education/healthcare to remote rural communities. Digital divide, potential loss of local community retail jobs.

02 E-Commerce Business Models & Concepts (Chapter 2)

Definition of a Business Model
A Business Model is a set of planned activities designed to result in a profit in a marketplace. A business plan documents this model, and an e-commerce business model leverages Internet unique qualities.

2.1 The 8 Key Elements of a Business Model

# Element Key Question Addressed Core Concept & Examples
1 Value Proposition Why should the customer buy from you? Defines how a product satisfies customer needs (convenience, lower price, personalization).
2 Revenue Model How will you earn money and generate profits? Advertising, Subscription, Transaction Fee, Sales, Affiliate models.
3 Market Opportunity What marketspace do you intend to serve, and what is its size? Realistic financial potential in the designated target niche.
4 Competitive Environment Who else occupies your intended marketspace? Direct competitors (similar products) and indirect competitors (substitute products).
5 Competitive Advantage What special advantages does your firm possess? First-mover advantages, brand prestige, lower cost structures, patents, network effects.
6 Market Strategy How do you plan to promote your products to attract customers? Pricing, promotion channels, social media campaigns, SEO, distribution.
7 Organizational Development What types of organizational structures are necessary? Department divisions, reporting hierarchies, and functional workflow allocation.
8 Management Team What experience and background are important for leaders? Crucial for venture capital; demonstrates operational competence and execution ability.

2.2 Major B2C Business Models

  • Portal: Offers search tools and integrated content packages. Horizontal Portals (Yahoo, MSN) cover diverse topics. Vertical Portals / Vortals (Sailnet, WebMD) focus on niche industries.
  • E-tailer: Online retail store. Includes Virtual Merchants (pure-play like Amazon/Wayfair), Clicks-and-Mortar (multi-channel like Walmart, Best Buy), Catalog Merchants (L.L. Bean), and Manufacturer-Direct (Dell, Apple).
  • Content Provider: Distributes digital content (news, music, video). Monetizes via subscriptions (Netflix), micropayments, syndication, and advertising (WSJ, CNN).
  • Transaction Broker: Processes online transactions (Expedia, E*Trade). Value proposition is saving consumers time and money through instant execution.
  • Market Creator: Uses Internet technology to create digital platforms where buyers and sellers meet, negotiate, and transact (eBay, Uber, Airbnb).
  • Service Provider: Sells online services (Google Maps, Carbonite cloud backups, Dropbox).
  • Community Provider: Creates digital environments where people with similar interests congregate, share content, and communicate (Facebook, LinkedIn, iVillage).

2.3 Major B2B Business Models

  • E-distributor: Supplies indirect MRO goods (maintenance, repair, and operations) directly to thousands of business buyers (e.g. W.W. Grainger).
  • E-procurement: Digital markets creating and selling access to digital procurement software (e.g. Ariba).
  • Exchanges (Net Marketplaces): Independently owned digital marketplaces connecting thousands of commercial suppliers and buyers for spot purchasing.
  • Industry Consortia: Industry-owned vertical markets owned by a small group of dominant purchasing enterprises.
  • Private Industrial Networks (PTX): Digital networks coordinating supply chain communications and collaborative design between a dominant firm (e.g. Walmart) and its key tier-1 suppliers.

03 Building an E-Commerce Presence: Websites, Mobile Sites, and Apps (Chapter 4)

Developing an e-commerce presence requires synthesizing business objectives, technical infrastructure, software engineering, and mobile responsiveness.

3.1 The Systems Development Life Cycle (SDLC) - 5 Steps

Step Phase Name Primary Activities & Deliverables
1 Systems Analysis / Planning Identify Business Objectives (what the site should do), System Functionalities (features needed), and Information Requirements (data elements required).
2 Systems Design Logical Design: Data flow diagrams (DFD), entity-relationship models, processing logic.
Physical Design: Hardware servers, software platforms, database engines, and telecommunications specs.
3 Building the System Decide between In-house build vs. Outsourcing development; choose hosting architecture (Self-hosting, Co-location, or Cloud/Managed Hosting).
4 Testing Unit Testing: Testing individual program modules.
System Testing: Testing the entire integrated multi-tier system under load.
Acceptance Testing: Verifying system satisfies client business requirements.
5 Implementation & Maintenance 24/7/365 site monitoring, continuous security patching, benchmarking, database reorganization, and ongoing feature updates. Dominates long-term lifetime costs.

3.2 Multi-Tier Architecture & Performance Optimization

  • Multi-Tier Hierarchy:
    • Tier 1 (Client): Browser or mobile app rendering HTML/CSS/JS.
    • Tier 2 (Web Server): Serves static assets, handles SSL/TLS termination, routes dynamic queries (e.g. Apache, NGINX).
    • Tier 3 (Application Server): Processes core business logic, shopping carts, taxes, and inventory workflows.
    • Tier 4 (Database Server): Manages relational/ACID data persistence, indexes, and transactions.
  • Optimization Strategies: Caching in memory (Redis, Memcached), Content Delivery Networks (CDNs like Cloudflare), database connection pooling, image compression, and minimizing HTTP round-trip requests.

3.3 Mobile Presence: Responsive Web Design vs. Native Apps

Approach Technology & Architecture Pros & Cons
Responsive Web Design (RWD) Fluid grid layouts, flexible CSS media queries. Single codebase for all devices. Pros: Cost-effective, universal reach, single maintenance URL.
Cons: Limited access to native phone hardware, requires persistent connection.
Native Mobile App Compiled platform code (Swift for iOS, Kotlin for Android). Pros: Peak performance, full hardware integration (GPS, camera, push notifications, biometrics), offline capabilities.
Cons: High development cost, requires app store approval and dual codebases.

04 E-Commerce Security and Payment Systems (Chapter 5)

4.1 The 6 Dimensions of E-Commerce Security

Security Dimension Customer's Perspective Merchant's Perspective
Integrity Has the information I transmitted or received been altered? Has data on the site or in the database been modified by unauthorized parties?
Non-repudiation Can a merchant deny having received my payment? Can a customer order goods and falsely deny having placed the order?
Authenticity Am I dealing with the real merchant and authentic website? What is the real identity of the customer making this purchase?
Confidentiality Can unauthorized third parties view my messages or credit card details? Are corporate proprietary trade secrets and consumer data protected from snooping?
Privacy Can I control the use and distribution of my personal profile data? Is personal information collected responsibly and compliant with privacy laws?
Availability Can I access the e-commerce website whenever I need to shop? Is the server infrastructure resilient against crashes, outages, and DoS attacks?

4.2 Major Security Threats

  • Malware: Viruses (require host file execution), Worms (self-replicating autonomous programs), Trojan Horses (disguised malicious programs), Ransomware (extortion via data encryption).
  • Botnets: Networks of compromised zombie computers used to launch massive Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks.
  • Phishing & Spoofing: Deceptive emails and counterfeit clone websites designed to steal credentials. Pharming redirects domain traffic directly.
  • Hacking: White hats (ethical security researchers), Black hats (malicious criminals), Grey hats (unauthorized researchers without criminal intent).
  • Sniffing & Man-in-the-Middle (MitM): Eavesdropping on unencrypted network traffic or intercepting communication streams.

4.3 Technology Solutions: Cryptography & PKI

Technology Mechanism Primary Application
Symmetric Encryption Single shared secret key used for both encryption and decryption (AES, DES). Fast. Bulk payload data encryption. Key distribution is the major challenge.
Asymmetric Encryption (Public Key) Two mathematically linked keys: Public Key (shared freely for encryption) and Private Key (secret, for decryption). Secure key exchange, digital signatures (RSA, ECC). Solves key distribution.
Digital Signature Message digest hashed and encrypted with the sender's Private Key. Guarantees Integrity and Non-repudiation. Verified using sender's public key.
Digital Certificate & CA Digital document issued by a trusted Certificate Authority (CA) binding a public key to an entity's verified identity. Guarantees Authenticity, enabling HTTPS/SSL/TLS communication.
SSL/TLS & HTTPS Combines asymmetric handshake for session key exchange and symmetric encryption for data stream. Secures client-server communication channels over the open Web.

4.4 Online Payment Systems & Protocols

  • Credit Card Transaction Flow: Consumer -> Merchant Gateway -> Acquiring Bank -> Card Brand Network (Visa/MC) -> Issuing Bank (authorizes fund transfer).
  • Digital Wallets (Apple Pay, PayPal, Google Pay): Store payment details securely. Modern wallets use Tokenization: replacing actual 16-digit card numbers with dynamic, one-time encrypted tokens.
  • 3D Secure (Verified by Visa, Mastercard Identity Check): Adds multi-factor authentication (OTP/biometrics) during checkout, shifting chargeback fraud liability to the card issuer.
  • PCI-DSS Compliance: Mandatory security standard governing all organizations handling cardholder data to protect against breaches.
